Two kids, a trash can that was always overflowing, and one question we couldn’t shake: where does all this food actually go? OmniCompost Home is our answer.

The bucket years

We were throwing away half our groceries — wilted greens, apple cores, the crusts nobody finished. It felt wrong, and honestly, it smelled wrong too. So we started composting on the patio. The kids named the worms. Dinner scraps became garden soil, and our tomatoes had never been happier.

The trouble was every bin we could buy was ugly, complicated, or built for a farm. We wanted one that fit a normal kitchen and a normal week — one a seven-year-old could help with. When we couldn’t find it, we built it.

📷 The Reyes family in the backyard, first harvest, 2019

What we figured out

A worm bin doesn’t have to be a chore. Ours eats up to five gallons of scraps a day, stays odor-free when it’s balanced, and is honestly kind of fun to feed. You can leave it for a two-week vacation and the worms are totally fine without you. We tested that part a lot.

Why we keep it honest

We’re a small family business, not a big factory, so we only tell you what our bin actually does in our own kitchen. No miracle claims. If something isn’t ready yet, we’ll just say so — that’s how we’d want to be talked to, and it’s how we raise our kids too.
