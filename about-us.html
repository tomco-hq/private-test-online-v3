About OmniCompost

The same promise, whoever happens to be making it.

OmniCompost is one worm-composting digester, built in small runs on the California coast. We would rather tell you plainly what it does — and what it can’t — than dress the work up. The promise on this page is the part that never changes hands, no matter whose hands build your bin.

Our company, and the person who makes it

I The promise, in four lines

i

The numbers are measured, not rounded up

Five gallons in a day. Two weeks unattended. Fourteen prototypes before this one. Every figure we publish was checked on a bench, not chosen for a headline.

ii

We tell you what it can’t do

It is not instant, not effortless, and not cheap to make well. If something isn’t ready, you’ll read that here before you read a claim about it.

iii

It ships slowly, on purpose

Short runs, checked one unit at a time. There is sometimes a wait. That isn’t a tactic — it’s just how long good assembly takes.

iv

The promise outlasts the maker

As OmniCompost grows, other hands will join the bench. These four lines will not change. That is the whole of the company’s marketing philosophy.

II Why we build it

When we tried to compost our own kitchen scraps, every bin we could buy was built for a garage or a farm — loud, ugly, or the size of a chest freezer. None of it was made to last, and none of it belonged in a kitchen. So we built the one we’d be proud to put our name on: powder-coated steel and beechwood, a brass tap for the tea, quiet and odorless when the colony is balanced, made to sit in the open for a decade rather than hide in a garage for a season.

It may be doubted whether there are many other animals which have played so important a part in the history of the world as these lowly organised creatures. Charles Darwin · on the earthworm, 1881

Five gallons of scraps in a single day. Most of it is water, and it leaves as water. What remains is castings — the quiet, finished thing a garden actually wants. The colony holds for two weeks with no food and no attention, a figure measured on a bench rather than rounded up for a page.

V Who makes it

Right now, that one person is Cornell Briggs. For forty years he built and mended boat hardware on the San Pedro waterfront — bronze fittings and marine steel that had to survive salt, sun, and a decade of weather without anyone thinking about them twice. The OmniCompost digester carries the same bronze-and-timber logic: a brass tap, powder-coated steel, and beechwood, assembled in short runs and checked one unit at a time.
